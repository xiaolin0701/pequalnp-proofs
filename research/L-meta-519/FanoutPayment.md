# A degree-three restriction step for a least circuit

Let a least clean binary AND/OR/NOT circuit compute a function \(f\). Fix an input \(v\) to \(b\) and suppose the restricted function is nonconstant. Let \(d\) count the distinct gates directly reading \(v\). For \(b=0\), let \(c\) count its direct AND readers. For \(b=1\), let \(c\) count its direct OR readers. Then this restriction saves at least \(d+c\) gates, at the same arity.

Start a set \(W\) with the \(c\) designated direct readers. Close it under gates reading two distinct outputs already in \(W\). Each new member is a binary gate. Every gate in \(W\) propagates the constant \(b\) under the chosen restriction. As a Boolean function \(h\) before restriction, it satisfies \(h\le v\) if \(b=0\) and \(v\le h\) if \(b=1\). Consequently no direct reader of \(v\) can read any output in \(W\). A binary gate combining comparable signals equals one of them, contradicting leastness. A unary gate cannot read two distinct wires.

Let \(m=|W|-c\) and let \(F\) be the set of consumers of \(W\) outside \(W\). Count ordered pairs consisting of a member and a gate reading its output. Each member has at least one consumer, since its output specializes to a constant and the final output is nonconstant. The \(c\) initial members consume no output of \(W\), the other \(m\) members consume two, and each member of \(F\) consumes at most one by closure. Thus \(c+m\le 2m+|F|\). The \(m+|F|\) gates consuming outputs of \(W\) are disjoint from all \(d\) direct readers. All these gates have an input that constant propagation recognizes as constant, and the least circuit restriction lemma deletes them. This proves the claim and also allows arbitrary fan-out inside \(W\).

For the edge function on a graph of maximum degree three, \(v=0\) removes its incident edges. The cover of the residual together with \(v\) covers the original graph, so the quantity \(|A|+|E|+\tau(G)\) decreases by at most four. A cubic vertex with \(d+c\ge4\) for \(b=0\) pays that step. Two direct readers both becoming constant at zero, including NOT, are covered separately by the upstream lemma `Least.fourth`. A NOT reader plus two other direct readers pays by `threeReaders` in [DegreeThreeZero.lean](DegreeThreeZero.lean).

There is also a paid positive restriction with three OR readers when the sum of the degrees of the three neighbors is at most six. Free those three neighbors and remove every edge incident to them and \(v\). These edges number at most the sum of the neighbor degrees, since the neighbors cover all the removed edges. Adding the three neighbors to a residual cover repairs the cover, while the new free inputs offset that increase. The potential loss is therefore at most six and the circuit drop is at least six. This argument uses that the original free inputs are disjoint from the endpoints of every edge.

The graph step with a four-gate zero drop and the three-reader mixed case are formalized in the linked Lean file. The arbitrary-fanout counting argument above is given as a mathematical proof. What remains in range zero is to show one may select a least circuit and a vertex with a paid restriction, or find a different paid restriction when all cubic vertices in the selected least circuit avoid the conditions above. In particular, one direct reader at a cubic vertex does not reach the four-gate zero threshold by this counting argument alone.

## A single AND reader with a private partner subcircuit

Let \(A=\varnothing\), let \(v\) have three neighbors in a graph of girth at least five, and let its only direct reader in a least clean full-basis circuit be \(g=v\land h\). Suppose the subcircuit for \(h\) is private: only \(g\) uses it outside that subcircuit. Whenever the residual has an edge, restricting \(v=0\) saves at least four gates. This also applies to any formula represented by a circuit whose gate outputs have fan-out at most one. Primary inputs may be used anywhere else.

For an independent set \(I\) avoiding \(v\) and containing a neighbor of \(v\), the function changes from zero to one when \(v\) is switched on. The wire \(h\) is independent of \(v\) and must be one on every such \(I\). The subcircuit for \(h\) contains at least two gates. To see this, a primary input or a single AND/OR gate on primary inputs depends on at most two of the three neighbors and is zero on the singleton consisting of an unmentioned neighbor. For a single NOT of a primary input \(z\), take the singleton \(z\) if \(z\) is a neighbor. If \(z\) is a nonneighbor, it can be adjacent to at most one neighbor of \(v\), by the absence of a four-cycle, and one can take \(I=\{z,j\}\) with a neighbor \(j\) not adjacent to \(z\). In each case the NOT candidate is zero on a required witness. The earliest possible constants are excluded by cleanliness and leastness.

At \(v=0\), the AND gate becomes zero. Its private partner subcircuit can be erased, and the AND gate plus one consumer simplify. Such a consumer exists, since the output is nonconstant under the restriction. These deletions are distinct and account for at least four gates. The statement extends to free inputs disjoint from edges: a candidate primary \(z\) that is itself free obstructs the two-point witness for a NOT, so this extension needs a further hypothesis or separate argument.
