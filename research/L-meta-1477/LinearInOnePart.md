# Circuits of line degree at most one for incidence

Let C be a monotone fan-in-two circuit for the order-four plane incidence function on 21 point variables and 21 line variables. Assign degree one to each line input and zero to each point input. The degree of an OR is the maximum of its children's degrees. The degree of an AND is the sum of its children's degrees, in agreement with PEqualNP.CircuitLowerBounds.Circuit.lineDeg.

**Proposition.** If the output has line degree at most one, C has at least 104 OR gates and at least 21 AND gates after normalizing constants and inputs. The same result holds with the two parts interchanged. The usual circuit with 21 five-input stars attains both counts.

## Linear network

Remove gates outside the output cone, fold constants and bypass unary gates. Every remaining AND that has positive degree multiplies a line-dependent wire by a point-only wire. Each degree-one wire can be written as a point-only summand together with an OR of terms each containing exactly one line variable and a point-only coefficient. Keep all point-only gates used as factors. In a positive-degree OR with one point-only input, drop that input and bypass the gate. The remaining positive-degree computation now consists of linear-wire sums and multiplications by a scalar point function. It computes exactly the original incidence function: the transformation preserves every line coefficient, and the original output's point-only summand vanishes at every point assignment because incidence is false when all lines are zero. These replacements, followed by removing dead gates, can only decrease each gate count. In particular all 21 line inputs still reach the output.

Call the number of linear-wire sums S and the number of linear-wire products P. Construct a point-only circuit for the 21 line coefficients simultaneously by starting with reverse coefficient one at the output. A sum sends its reverse coefficient to each operand; a product sends the reverse coefficient times the scalar factor to the linear operand. Where multiple contributions reach a wire take their OR. Induction in reverse topological order identifies the reverse coefficient with the OR of the products of the scalar factors on all paths from that wire to the output. At each primary line input this is exactly the OR of its five incident point variables. This construction reuses the original point-only subcircuit and all new products are AND gates. There are 2S+P directed linear-wire operand occurrences. They are shared among 21+S+P−1 useful linear vertices other than the output. Each receives at least one occurrence, and a vertex with d occurrences takes at most d−1 binary ORs to collect its contributions. Thus there are at most S−20 new ORs. If A is the number of original point-only OR gates, the reverse circuit computes all 21 line ORs using at most A+S−20 ORs.

## Simultaneous line outputs require 84 OR gates

For a line B evaluate every gate at the point assignment with exactly the points outside B set true. The output for B is false. Construct a reverse subgraph from this output, keeping both children of ORs and choosing one false child at each AND. Reached wires are all false at the outside assignment. Therefore a reached wire accepts no singleton outside B, by monotonicity.

Let the singleton support of a wire be the set of the point variables on which the wire accepts a singleton. Stop the reverse traversal as soon as the support has size at most one. At an OR the support is the union of both operands' supports; at an AND the chosen operand's support contains the parent support. Thus every point of B is represented among the terminal supports. There are at least five terminals. In the connected reverse subgraph every internal vertex has at most one child except for the binary OR gates, so at least four binary OR gates are needed. Each of these OR gates has singleton support of size at least two contained in B. No gate counted this way can be counted for a different line because two different lines intersect in just one point. The joint circuit needs at least 21·4=84 OR gates. Consequently A+S≥104 in the original circuit.

## First products require 21 AND gates

Fix a singleton point input p. A line input l reaches the true output exactly when there is a path of sums and products from l to the output along which every scalar factor is true at p. No product-free path can reach the output from a line input, since it would also accept that line when there are no points. On each accepting path designate its first product g. The set of singleton pairs admitting such a path factors as a rectangle. Its line side consists of line inputs reaching g's linear operand through sums alone. Its point side consists of points at which g's factor is true and some path from g to the output has all subsequent factors true. Concatenating the prefix and suffix realizes every cross pair, so the rectangle is contained in the incidence graph. This graph has no four-cycle and degree five, so every rectangle covers at most five edges. The 105 edges require at least 21 distinct first products, proving P≥21. Adding the disjoint gate counts yields 125.
