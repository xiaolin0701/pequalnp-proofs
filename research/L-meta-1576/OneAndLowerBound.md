# One AND gate requires at least 39 gates on the F01 domain

Number the points of the projective plane by `Fin 13` and write `L_t={t,t+1,t+3,t+9}` with arithmetic modulo 13. The tests in the question require all thirteen line ORs whenever point 0 is true, point 1 is true, or every point outside `L_0` is true. Suppose at most one gate is AND. Then at least 39 gates are required. A circuit using exclusively OR needs exactly 39 gates.

Every pair of distinct points belongs to one line, as can be checked by taking the twelve ordered, nonzero differences within `L_0={0,1,3,9}`. In particular, `L_0` is the unique line through both distinguished points. Each complement of a line is an allowed input. For each line and each point `p` on it, an allowed input exists with `p` its sole true point on that line. For a line missing one distinguished point, add that point to `p`. For `L_0`, add all points outside `L_0`.

Consequently, every OR-only wire that computes a line output on the required domain computes its exact OR on every input. Testing the line complement forbids outside variables, and the private input for `p` forces every line variable to appear. An OR-only root depending on four inputs needs three gates with support at least two. To see this, collapse copy gates, constants and gates with singleton support, then count edges in the connected binary circuit feeding the root. Such a gate cannot feed two different line outputs, because their intersection has only one point. Thus `13 × 3=39` gates are necessary, and separate three-gate trees attain equality.

An invalid forward reference evaluates to false. Propagate these constants along with nullary constants and bypass unary copies. These simplifications never add gates. Suppose the remaining AND computes `h = (⋁ A) ∧ (⋁ B)`, where `A,B` are nonempty sets of original variables. Every downstream output has the form `(⋁ U_t) ∨ h` or `⋁ U_t`. The complement test forces `U_t ⊆ L_t`. For any output containing `h`, that test also forces `A ⊆ L_t` or `B ⊆ L_t`.

If one operand has at least two variables, name that operand `B`. Replace the AND by the earlier wire `⋁ A`, thereby deleting one gate. Every affected output on a line containing `A` stays correct on the domain because its value increases and all its variables lie on the line. An exceptional affected line must contain all of `B`. There is at most one such line. Reconstruct its exact output from the already available wire `⋁ B`, adding the remaining at most two variables with at most two OR gates. If the exceptional original output is the AND itself, every private point test forces that point into the line-contained operand. Thus this operand already equals the whole line and no new gate is needed. Otherwise the exceptional output is an OR strictly downstream of the AND. It cannot reach any other line output because all downstream gates are OR gates, and the complement of the other line is an allowed input on which the exceptional line is true and the other is false. The old output gate can therefore also be deleted when rebuilding the exception. The deletion of the AND and this private OR pays for both new ORs. This gives a full OR-only circuit no larger than the original.

It remains to consider `A={a}` and `B={b}`. The case `a=b` is a copy. For `a ≠ b`, remove the gate by assigning its output false and propagating constants. A point omitted from the remaining set `U_t` must be supplied by `h` on every private test for that point. A line containing both `a,b` loses nothing, since `h` is false on all its private tests. Consider a line containing `a` but excluding `b`. Only point `a` can be omitted. If `a` is distinguished, the allowed singleton `a` prevents any omission. If neither distinguished point lies on the line, the two private tests `{a,0}` and `{a,1}` would both have to include `b`, which is impossible. If exactly one distinguished point is on the line, the private test using the other forces that other point to equal `b`. This determines the line uniquely from `a` and the distinguished point it contains. The remaining possibility is `L_0`, which can arise only if `b` is not distinguished. Thus at most one line containing `a` but excluding `b` needs repair, and that repair adds `a` by one OR. In fact the opposite orientation cannot require a second repair. A distinguished coordinate can never be omitted. If both coordinates are undistinguished, each orientation could only require a repair on the unique line through both distinguished points, and that line cannot at once contain just the first and just the second coordinate. Hence at most one OR is added in total. Deleting the AND pays for that OR.

This argument also shows that a realization of the official instance with at most 38 gates, if one exists, must contain at least two AND gates. The construction using 39 OR gates attains the bound for circuits allowed up to one AND.

## Peeling later AND gates

The argument with a wide operand also works when earlier AND gates are present. Suppose the final AND gate has an operand computing exactly the OR of a set B of at least two distinct original inputs. Let its other operand be the monotone function u. Every wire downstream of this final AND is built only by OR gates, so increasing the AND's output to u can only increase the affected outputs. At an affected line t, the complement test gives h(off t) = 0. If u(off t) = 0, then by monotonicity u accepts only inputs hitting the line, and the replacement preserves correctness there. Otherwise the B operand is false on that complement, forcing B to be contained in the line. Again at most one exceptional line occurs. Use the same repair and remove its private output gate. When the exceptional output is the AND itself, its private point tests force B to equal the whole line. Thus the replacement gives another correct circuit of no larger size and with one fewer AND.

In particular, if every AND after the earliest one has such an operand, the exact minimum is still 39. Any circuit using at most 38 gates must have a later AND for which neither operand computes the OR of two or more distinct original inputs.
